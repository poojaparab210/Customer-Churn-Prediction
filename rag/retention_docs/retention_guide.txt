
CUSTOMER RETENTION GUIDE

1. Month-to-month contracts
When a customer has a high churn risk score and a month-to-month contract,
investigate their concerns about pricing, service quality, or contract flexibility.
Explain available contract options transparently. Never pressure a customer
into a longer contract.

2. Short customer tenure
Customers who recently joined may benefit from onboarding guidance, help
understanding their plan, and proactive support. Ask whether they have unresolved
setup, service, or billing questions.

3. Technical support
If a customer reports technical problems, offer troubleshooting or explain
available support options. Do not assume that a customer is dissatisfied
simply because they do not subscribe to a support service.

4. Billing concerns
Review bills when customers report unexpected charges. Explain fees clearly
and discuss available payment methods when relevant. Never promise a refund
or discount without authorization.

5. Customer feedback
Ask customers about their main concern before recommending a retention action.
Record the concern, the action offered, and the outcome.

6. Responsible churn prediction
A churn model estimates risk; it cannot establish why a customer might leave.
Use predictions to prioritize review rather than as proof. Protect customer
information and avoid discriminatory treatment.

7. Measuring retention
Track retention, accepted offers, complaints, and customer satisfaction.
Do not claim that a retention action works unless evaluation data supports it.
